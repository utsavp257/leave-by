The thing nobody tells you

The same seventeen miles, half again as long

In 2015 Todd Schneider measured this exact trip — Midtown to JFK, leaving between ten and eleven in the morning — across a billion taxi records. It took 38 minutes. The road has not moved. The trip has.

One more thing

The shuttle is a price answer, not a safety one

Uber Shuttle runs fixed routes to all three airports for a fraction of a car fare, and it is a genuinely good deal. It is not a genuinely safer bet.

It drives the same roads as every other car, so it draws its travel time from the same distribution — and it inherits the whole tail. Fixed stops and a wait of up to half an hour can only add to it. Cheaper, yes. More reliable, no.

There is no public trip data for it, so this is reasoning from the road network rather than measurement. It is stated because "just take the shuttle" circulates as general airport advice, and it is advice about your wallet being mistaken for advice about your flight.

How this was measured. Car times come from the Taxi & Limousine Commission's trip records — every high-volume for-hire and yellow cab trip, weekdays only, as percentiles over whole minutes. Train times come from observed subway movements, with the walk, the wait and the AirTrain or Q70 leg modelled rather than measured, and combined so that percentiles are convolved rather than added.

Because that combination is done conservatively, the transit figures are slightly pessimistic. Where the train wins, it wins by at least as much as shown.

LaGuardia's bus leg is averaged by the MTA over a month, a weekday and an hour before publication, which hides how bad the worst buses are.

Newark is car only, and not for want of a railway. New Jersey Transit and Amtrak both call at Newark Airport Station, about 24 minutes from Penn Station. The problem is the last mile: the AirTrain there is being replaced in a project running to 2030, and through it the shuttle is a bus on most weekday mornings, paused over summer and the holidays, resuming in between. A journey time published today would describe a trip that no longer exists next month. New Jersey Transit also reports punctuality as a percentage rather than as journey times, so even the rail leg has no distribution behind it.

New Jersey origins are absent for a different reason — the taxi zone system covers the five boroughs, so a trip beginning in Jersey City is not in these records at all.
